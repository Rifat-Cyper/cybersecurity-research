As digital ecosystems expand beyond traditional computing systems, the scope of cyber forensic investigations must evolve accordingly. Mobile devices, Internet of Things (IoT) endpoints, virtual machines, and cloud infrastructures have become key environments where critical evidence resides. Unlike conventional hard drives or on-premises servers, these platforms present unique challenges related to data volatility, distributed storage, and jurisdictional complexity. This report explores forensic acquisition and analysis techniques adapted to these modern environments. Mobile forensics now requires tools that can extract data from encrypted, cloud-synced applications, while IoT investigations demand methods for capturing fragmented logs from transient devices. Cloud forensics emphasizes issues such as multi-tenancy, access control, and data replication across global infrastructures. Virtual machine environments introduce complications in isolating evidence due to snapshot layering and shared hypervisors. Drawing upon established frameworks and current academic literature, this report outlines the limitations of existing forensic models and presents a multi-layered approach to digital evidence preservation. The aim is to enhance investigative readiness, ensure evidentiary integrity, and support cross-domain collaboration in modern forensic scenarios.

## Introduction

In recent years, the rapid proliferation of non-traditional computing platforms—namely mobile devices, Internet of Things (IoT) systems, virtual machines (VMs), and cloud infrastructures has transformed the landscape of digital forensic investigations. These environments generate and store vast volumes of sensitive data that are increasingly targeted in cybercrimes, yet they fall outside the reach of conventional forensic frameworks developed for physical desktops and local servers. Traditional methods of digital evidence acquisition and interpretation are often insufficient in dynamic and distributed architectures, particularly where real-time data, remote storage, and cross-jurisdictional access are involved. For instance, mobile devices now rely heavily on encrypted applications and cloud synchronization, making evidence acquisition highly contextual and technically demanding (Iqbal et al., 2023). Similarly, IoT ecosystem characterized by lightweight, autonomous, and transient devices lack centralized logging mechanisms, complicating both acquisition and validation of digital evidence (Solangi et al., 2022). Cloud environments introduce additional concerns related to data ownership, shared infrastructure, and multi-tenancy, all of which affect the admissibility and traceability of evidence (Makura et al., 2020).Furthermore, virtual machines complicate the preservation of evidence due to their dynamic nature, frequent snapshotting, and virtualization layers. These factors necessitate a shift from reactive to proactive forensic models that account for volatility, data replication, and layered access controls. This report aims to evaluate the current limitations of forensic methods when applied to mobile, IoT, VM, and cloud contexts. It also introduces adaptive strategies to improve evidence acquisition, enhance forensic readiness, and ensure procedural integrity across these non-traditional platforms.

## Audience , Management in Accounting, Legal, and Technology

This report is specifically designed to serve the needs of senior professionals across three critical departments: accounting, legal, and technology. Each of these domains plays a distinct yet interconnected role in the identification, interpretation, and escalation of digital evidence arising from non-traditional computing environments. For accounting managers, the ability to trace and validate financial data through cloud platforms, mobile applications, or IoT-enabled payment systems is increasingly vital. Financial records no longer reside solely on internal servers; instead, they are often distributed across virtual machines or encrypted mobile apps. This decentralization necessitates a forensic approach capable of validating transactions, identifying anomalies, and supporting audit integrity using digital evidence drawn from diverse platforms (Iqbal et al., 2023).From the legal perspective, the chain of custody and evidentiary admissibility are paramount. As digital evidence becomes more volatile and fragmented particularly in cloud and IoT ecosystems legal departments must understand the technical limitations and procedural requirements for presenting such evidence in court. This report outlines the safeguards and documentation standards needed to align technical investigations with legal frameworks (Makura et al., 2020).For IT and cybersecurity teams, this report provides a roadmap for evidence acquisition, system monitoring, and proactive forensic readiness. It outlines the critical differences between traditional and modern environments, and introduces frameworks for capturing ephemeral data, interpreting virtual machine snapshots, and logging user behavior across distributed platforms (Solangi et al., 2022). By bridging the concerns of these three departments, this report promotes an integrated response to cyber incidents that enhances operational transparency, reduces risk, and supports legal and financial compliance.

## Conclusion

The shift toward non-traditional computing environments mobile devices, IoT systems, virtual machines, and cloud platforms has redefined the digital forensic landscape. These systems, while efficient and scalable, pose new challenges that impact the ability to reliably collect, preserve, and interpret digital evidence. Forensic investigations are no longer confined to physical hard drives or local networks; they must now address evidence that is transient, encrypted, decentralized, and often located across legal jurisdictions. From an accounting standpoint, this evolution introduces complexities in transaction validation and fraud detection. Financial data stored in mobile applications or distributed cloud databases lacks the audit trails traditionally relied upon for compliance and accuracy (Iqbal et al., 2023). For legal departments, the volatility and inaccessibility of digital evidence especially in shared cloud environments or IoT logs threatens the integrity of the chain of custody and raises questions about admissibility in court (Makura et al., 2020).For technical and cybersecurity teams, the implications are equally significant. Conventional forensic tools must evolve to accommodate new acquisition methods for encrypted devices, volatile memory in virtual machines, and log data in IoT ecosystems. Failure to do so may result in incomplete investigations, loss of critical evidence, and regulatory noncompliance (Solangi et al., 2022).This report highlights the need for integrated forensic frameworks that bridge legal, financial, and technical domains. Organizations must invest in training, proactive readiness, and adaptive tools to meet the evidentiary demands of modern digital ecosystems. Only through a coordinated and forward-thinking approach can institutions ensure that digital evidence retains its value and reliability in complex investigative scenarios
